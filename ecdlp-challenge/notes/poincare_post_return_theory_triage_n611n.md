## Theory 1: Global Cartier completion of the determinant

### Core mechanism

Extend the N609J bordered determinant through the true-pole, origin, and
nonrational charts using the N610J/N610N/N610O/N610Q transition identities.
The resulting global H018 Cartier member would give a canonical projective
model `C_t` whose normalization can be computed rather than inferred.

### Why it might evade the return barrier

This is a conservative construction step, not a new compression claim.  It
makes available divisor classes on `C_t` that are invisible in the raw pair
labels screened by N611M.

### Minimal test

Write the true-pole and regular-complement transition functions as actual
regular units on a common finite chart cover, verify every Cech triple, and
compare the resulting Cartier divisor class to H018.  A formal coefficient or
finite-point replay is insufficient.

### Likely failure

The local formal factors may not extend as regular units on nonrational
overlaps.  That would yield a precise obstruction to the current determinant
model, not to all compactifications or normalization approaches.

### What is learned if it fails

The failed overlap identifies the exact divisor correction or reflexive-module
data a replacement section must carry.

## Theory 2: Prym packet orthogonal to canonical returns

### Core mechanism

After a genuine normalization is available, compute the two induced maps from
`Jac(C_t)` to `E` and form the connected kernel/Prym-type component.  Seek
packet labels represented by divisors in that component, together with an
explicit target-return map not in the N611M projection span.

### Why it might evade the return barrier

N611M only exhausts fixed returns generated by the two visible elliptic
projections.  A nontrivial complementary abelian component could carry
different divisor geometry.

### Minimal test

Materialize `C_t`, normalize it, compute its genus and the degrees of both
elliptic maps, then calculate the finite-field order and isogeny factors of
the induced Jacobian.  Reject immediately if every available return to `E`
factors through the screened projection span.

### Likely failure

The Jacobian may decompose only into components whose maps to `E` are
canonical, or its factor base may still have base-field-scale support.

### What is learned if it fails

The decomposition supplies a restricted return theorem for this exact member
and distinguishes a representation failure from a relation-density failure.

## Theory 3: Secant-divisor relation packets on the normalized member

### Core mechanism

Use intersections of fixed low-degree divisors on the normalized `C_t` to form
small effective divisor packets.  Test whether their Abel-Jacobi sums admit a
source inverse and a relation alphabet smaller than the raw complete-level
source set.

### Why it might evade the return barrier

The packet lives on divisor classes of one curve rather than pointwise maps
from `E x E` to `E`, so it is not a relabeling covered by N611M.

### Minimal test

On a proven global model, enumerate a fixed divisor family and compare its
exact relation support against randomized divisors of equal degree.  Require a
target embedding, rank, individual-log descent, and charged cost before
collecting a factor-base matrix.

### Likely failure

The divisor family may have random-like support, or every target return may
collapse into the canonical span.

### What is learned if it fails

The result bounds a concrete normalized-divisor family and leaves other curve
members and non-divisor representations open.
