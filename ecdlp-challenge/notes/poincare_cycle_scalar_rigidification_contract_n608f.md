# Experiment Contract: N608F Poincare full-cycle scalar-rigidification audit

## Hypothesis

The N607A finite-orbit strictification may arise from an evaluation-independent
scalar rigidification of the N606Z Poincare transport on a common regular
domain.

## Null hypothesis

The full-cycle product of the 109 Miller transport factors depends on the
evaluation point. Then one scalar 109th-root correction can strictify one
chosen evaluation point but cannot define an evaluation-independent scalar
rigidification of the moving frame.

## Parameters

- N606Y/N606Z H018 toy curve `E/F_103`;
- base-shift orbit of length 109 generated by `[84]G`;
- common regular points in `E(F_(103^2))`;
- full-cycle product of all 109 explicitly evaluated Miller transport factors.

## Metrics

- number of common regular extension-field points sampled;
- number of distinct nonzero full-cycle products;
- direct replay of N607A's one-point strictification precondition;
- independence boundary between a global scalar correction and a
  point-dependent or matrix-valued bundle map.

## Positive control

At each sampled point, the full product is nonzero and has a unique 109th root
in `F_(103^2)^*` because `gcd(109,103^2-1)=1`.

## Falsification criterion

Two distinct full-cycle products on the common regular domain reject an
evaluation-independent scalar correction. They do not reject a point-dependent
frame gauge, normalized Poincare line-bundle data, or an explicit
matrix-valued pushforward intertwiner.


## Claim boundary

`NEGATIVE RESULT / SCALAR-RIGIDIFICATION-ONLY / MODEL-BOUND / TOY-EVIDENCE /
NO_ECDLP_CLAIM`.

## Results

The first 12 common regular `F_(103^2)` evaluations all produce nonzero but
pairwise-distinct 109-edge products. The finite-field root condition still
holds at every point, so N607A can strictify each individual evaluation but no
single scalar works across this common domain.

## Interpretation

This rejects only an evaluation-independent scalar correction of the N606Z
moving-frame transport. A point-dependent normalized Poincare trivialization
or a matrix-valued pushforward intertwiner is still the appropriate positive
construction target.
